# HealthyOne content guidelines

Version 1, 2026-11-11. One page. Every HealthyOne blog post, written by a
person or by a script, follows these rules. Our health advisors approve this
page once; after that, a post only carries a "reviewed by" line when that
advisor has read that post and said OK.

## What we stand for
HealthyOne helps people get healthier with food, movement, sleep and steady
habits. We meet people who use weight-loss medication where they are, but we
lead with natural, everyday changes that anyone can keep doing for life.

## The four kinds of posts (lanes)
1. **Eating for natural weight loss.** Whole-food meal plans, high-protein and
   high-fiber food lists, grocery lists, cooking basics, eating out, holidays.
2. **Movement.** Walking plans, beginner strength training at home, keeping
   muscle while losing weight.
3. **Appetite and cravings without medication.** Protein, fiber, sleep, stress
   and meal timing. Only effects that are well established.
4. **Life after GLP-1 (the bridge).** Keeping weight off without medication,
   and habits to build while on it. At most about 1 post in 5. This is the
   only lane where drug names (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound and others)
   may appear, in the title or anywhere else.

## Medication: how we talk about it
- We never tell anyone to start, stop, skip, lower or change a medication.
- Any question about a medication, dose or side effect ends with
  "talk to your prescriber" (or "your doctor").
- We don't attack medication or the people who use it. We say what food,
  movement and sleep can do, and leave the medical decision to them and
  their doctor.
- We don't promote prescription services or telehealth companies.

## What we never claim
- No "cures", "guaranteed results", "melts fat", "detox" or "cleanse".
- No supplement as a drug substitute: no "natural Ozempic", "nature's
  Ozempic", berberine, "GLP-1 boosting" pills or teas.
- No made-up studies, statistics, quotes or experts. If we use a number, it
  is a well-known, widely published figure (for example, general fiber or
  sleep guidelines), stated plainly and roughly.
- No promises of how much weight someone will lose or how fast.
- No "reviewed by" line unless that reviewer approved that exact post.

## Food safety basics
- Portions and protein grams are rough guides for healthy adults, not a
  prescription.
- Anyone who is pregnant, breastfeeding, under 18, has diabetes, kidney
  disease, an eating disorder or another medical condition should check
  eating changes with their doctor or a registered dietitian.
- No very-low-calorie plans, fasting challenges or skipping whole food groups.
- Add fiber slowly and with water. Cook meat, eggs and leftovers safely.

## Exercise safety basics
- Start easy and build up slowly. Something is better than nothing.
- Anyone with heart, lung, joint or other health problems, or who has been
  inactive for a long time, should talk to a doctor before starting.
- Stop if something hurts (sharp pain, chest pain, dizziness) and get it
  checked.
- Show simple moves that need no gym, and an easier version of each.

## Tone
- Plain, direct and kind. Short paragraphs. Specific numbers (grams,
  portions, minutes) instead of vague advice.
- No shame, no "before and after" pressure, no fear tactics.
- Practical lists and plans people can use today.
- HealthyOne gets one short, honest section near the end, never a hard sell.

## Who checks this
Our health advisors (a registered dietitian and a medical doctor) approve
these guidelines. Each week they get a short summary of new posts with every
health claim listed. A post gets a "Nutrition reviewed by" or "Medically
reviewed by" line only after that advisor replies OK.
