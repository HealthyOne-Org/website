Mounjaro Review 2026: Weight Loss, Cost, Mounjaro vs Zepbound

October 20, 2026 · 6 min read · By Chris Hardaway
Photo via Pexels

Mounjaro was the first tirzepatide product, approved in 2022 for type 2 diabetes, and for about a year and a half it was the only way to get the molecule. Then Zepbound arrived with the obesity label and most of the weight-loss conversation moved there. That leaves Mounjaro in a specific spot in 2026: the right drug for people with type 2 diabetes who also need to lose weight, and the wrong door for everyone else.

What Mounjaro Is and Who It Is For

Mounjaro is a once-weekly injection of tirzepatide, which activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. It is FDA-approved to improve blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes, alongside diet and exercise. Doses run from 2.5 mg up to 15 mg, stepping up every four weeks or more. It is not approved for weight loss, even though it produces a lot of it, and that gap between what the drug does and what the label says is the whole story of getting it covered.

What It Did in Trials

  • Blood sugar (SURPASS trials): large reductions in A1C across doses, with many participants reaching a non-diabetic A1C range, and better results than comparators including semaglutide 1 mg and insulin in the respective trials.
  • Weight in people with diabetes: roughly 7 to 12 kg lost depending on dose in the SURPASS program, consistently more than the comparator drugs.
  • Weight in people without diabetes: the same molecule, studied as Zepbound, averaged about 20 percent body-weight loss at 15 mg over 72 weeks in SURMOUNT-1, and beat Wegovy head-to-head in SURMOUNT-5.

One pattern holds across every GLP-1: people with type 2 diabetes lose somewhat less weight than people without it on the same drug. It is still a lot.

Cost and the Insurance Reality

Mounjaro's list price is around $1,080 a month. If you have type 2 diabetes and commercial insurance, it is usually covered with prior authorization, and Lilly's savings card can bring the copay down to as little as $25 subject to its terms. Medicare Part D plans generally cover it for diabetes. If you do not have diabetes, two things are true: insurers will almost always deny it, and Lilly's self-pay LillyDirect pricing of $299 to $449 applies to Zepbound, not Mounjaro. In that case the Zepbound review is the one you want.

Side Effects

Identical to Zepbound, because it is the same drug: nausea around dose increases, constipation that can linger, reflux, fatigue, reduced appetite. For people with diabetes there is one addition worth knowing: if you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea, the combination can cause low blood sugar, and your prescriber may adjust those doses. Rarer serious risks on the label include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease and a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents. Food can manage the common ones; anything else is a prescriber conversation.

Eating on Mounjaro

Two goals pull in the same direction here. Blood-sugar control wants fewer refined carbs and more protein and fiber. Muscle preservation during fast weight loss wants protein first and a daily floor near 100 grams. Both want small, steady meals rather than one big one that a slowed stomach cannot handle. How People Actually Get Mounjaro in 2026

There are three routes, and the right one depends on your insurance and your diagnosis.

  1. Through your own doctor and insurance. Mounjaro is labeled for type 2 diabetes, so coverage generally requires that diagnosis. Ask your prescriber what your plan requires; prior authorization is common.
  2. Self-pay direct from the manufacturer. Lilly does not offer a cash-pay Mounjaro program comparable to LillyDirect's Zepbound pricing; cash payers without diabetes are usually steered to Zepbound. Prices change often, so check the manufacturer's page before you decide.
  3. Through an online weight-loss clinic. A licensed clinician reviews your history by video or questionnaire, prescribes if appropriate, and either sends the prescription to a pharmacy or ships treatment to you. This is the fastest route if your regular doctor will not prescribe for weight loss.

FAQ

Is Mounjaro the same as Zepbound?

Yes. Both are tirzepatide made by Eli Lilly at the same 2.5 to 15 mg doses. Mounjaro is FDA-approved for type 2 diabetes; Zepbound is approved for obesity and obstructive sleep apnea. The molecule does not know which box it came in, but your insurer does.

How much weight do people lose on Mounjaro?

In the SURPASS diabetes trials, weight loss ran roughly 7 to 12 kg depending on dose. In people without diabetes, the same molecule produced about 20 percent body-weight loss at 15 mg in SURMOUNT-1. People with type 2 diabetes tend to lose somewhat less than people without it on every GLP-1.

Can I get Mounjaro for weight loss without diabetes?

A prescriber can write it off-label, but insurance almost never covers it for weight alone and Lilly's LillyDirect self-pay pricing applies to Zepbound, not Mounjaro. If you do not have type 2 diabetes, Zepbound is the practical version of the same drug.

What does Mounjaro cost in 2026?

The list price is around $1,080 a month. With commercial insurance that covers it for type 2 diabetes, the Mounjaro savings card can bring the copay to as little as $25, subject to its terms. Without diabetes coverage, the cash route is Zepbound through LillyDirect at $299 to $449.

The Bottom Line

Mounjaro is Zepbound with a diabetes label. If you have type 2 diabetes, it is one of the most effective drugs available for both blood sugar and weight, and it is the version your insurance is built to cover. If you do not, Zepbound is the same molecule through the door that is actually open. Either way, the appetite drop is strong, the under-eating risk is real, and protein-first eating is what keeps the weight you lose from being muscle.
