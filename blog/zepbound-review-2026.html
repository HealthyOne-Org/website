Zepbound Review 2026: Results, LillyDirect Price, Side Effects

October 20, 2026 · 7 min read · By Chris Hardaway
Photo via Pexels

Zepbound is tirzepatide, Eli Lilly's once-weekly injection that hits two gut-hormone receptors (GLP-1 and GIP) instead of one. It is the drug behind the biggest weight-loss numbers in any approved medication's trials, it won the only head-to-head test against Wegovy, and since Lilly started selling vials direct, it is the one most cash-paying patients ask about. This review is the plain version: results, cost by dose, side effects, and the food side that the pen does not handle.

What Zepbound Is and Who It Is For

Zepbound is a weekly self-injection, available as a single-dose pen or, through LillyDirect, as a vial you draw up with a syringe. Dosing starts at 2.5 mg and steps up every four weeks or more to a maintenance dose between 5 and 15 mg. It is FDA-approved for adults with obesity or with overweight plus a weight-related condition, and separately for moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity. Mounjaro is the same molecule labeled for type 2 diabetes; our Mounjaro review covers that side.

What It Did in Trials

  • SURMOUNT-1 (72 weeks, no diabetes): average loss of about 15 percent on 5 mg, 19.5 percent on 10 mg and 20.9 percent on 15 mg, versus about 3 percent on placebo.
  • SURMOUNT-5 (72 weeks, vs Wegovy 2.4 mg): 20.2 percent on Zepbound versus 13.7 percent on Wegovy. About 65 percent of the Zepbound group lost at least 15 percent of their body weight; 32 percent lost a quarter. Waist circumference dropped about 7 inches versus 5.

Those are the best numbers any approved weight-loss drug has produced. Two honest footnotes. Wegovy's 7.2 mg HD dose, approved in 2026, reached about 20.7 percent in its own trial, so the gap at the top is smaller than SURMOUNT-5 suggests. And an average of 20 percent includes people who lost 8 and people who lost 30. The difference between them is usually food, sleep and muscle, not the pen.

Cost in 2026: LillyDirect Changed the Math

The list price is about $1,086 a month. Almost nobody pays that. With commercial insurance that covers Zepbound, the savings card can bring it to $25 a month subject to annual caps. Without coverage, LillyDirect's self-pay program prices vials and KwikPens at $299 for 2.5 mg, $399 for 5 mg, and $449 for 7.5 through 15 mg as long as you refill within 45 days; miss the window and the higher doses revert to $499 and $699. Medicare Part D members may qualify for a $50 copay on the KwikPen through the GLP-1 Bridge program that started July 1, 2026. Prices move; check LillyDirect before you commit.

Side Effects That Actually Come Up

The list matches every GLP-1: nausea around dose increases, reflux, fatigue, food turning unappealing. The one people underestimate on tirzepatide is constipation, which tends to linger past the step-up weeks and needs fiber, fluid and sometimes a conversation with your prescriber. The GLP-1 constipation guide covers the food side. The label also lists rarer serious risks, including pancreatitis, gallbladder disease and a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents. Nothing here is a reason to adjust a dose on your own.

The Eating Problem Zepbound Creates

Because the appetite suppression is stronger than on semaglutide for most people, the under-eating problem is also bigger. It is common to finish a day on Zepbound at 600 or 700 calories and 40 grams of protein without noticing, because nothing felt like hunger. That is how people lose muscle alongside fat and end up lighter but weaker. The fix is mechanical: protein first at every meal, five small eating windows instead of three, and a daily floor around 100 grams. How People Actually Get Zepbound in 2026

There are three routes, and the right one depends on your insurance and your diagnosis.

  1. Through your own doctor and insurance. Zepbound is labeled for obesity and for obstructive sleep apnea, and the sleep apnea indication sometimes unlocks coverage that obesity alone does not. Ask your prescriber what your plan requires; prior authorization is common.
  2. Self-pay direct from the manufacturer. LillyDirect sells Zepbound vials and KwikPens at the $299 to $449 self-pay tiers with home delivery. Prices change often, so check the manufacturer's page before you decide.
  3. Through an online weight-loss clinic. A licensed clinician reviews your history by video or questionnaire, prescribes if appropriate, and either sends the prescription to a pharmacy or ships treatment to you. This is the fastest route if your regular doctor will not prescribe for weight loss.

FAQ

How much weight do people lose on Zepbound?

In SURMOUNT-1, adults without diabetes lost about 15 percent on 5 mg, 19.5 percent on 10 mg and 20.9 percent on 15 mg over 72 weeks. In the SURMOUNT-5 head-to-head trial, Zepbound averaged 20.2 percent versus 13.7 percent for Wegovy 2.4 mg.

How much does Zepbound cost through LillyDirect?

As of October 2026, self-pay vials and KwikPens are $299 for 2.5 mg, $399 for 5 mg, and $449 for 7.5 through 15 mg when you refill within 45 days. Without the refill discount the higher doses are $499 and $699. The list price is about $1,086. Commercially insured patients with coverage may pay as little as $25 with the savings card.

Is Zepbound the same as Mounjaro?

Yes, both are tirzepatide from Eli Lilly at the same doses. Mounjaro is labeled for type 2 diabetes; Zepbound is labeled for obesity and obstructive sleep apnea. Insurance follows the label, and LillyDirect's self-pay pricing applies to Zepbound.

What are the worst Zepbound side effects?

Nausea, constipation, diarrhea, reflux and fatigue, mostly around dose increases. Constipation tends to be more persistent on tirzepatide than people expect. Rarer serious risks on the label include pancreatitis and gallbladder disease, plus a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors in rodents. Talk to your prescriber about anything beyond food management.

The Bottom Line

Zepbound is the strongest approved weight-loss medication by the trial numbers, it beat Wegovy head-to-head, and LillyDirect turned a $1,086 list price into $299 to $449 cash. The tradeoffs are a weekly injection, constipation that needs managing, and a price that is still higher than the pills. If your prescriber agrees it fits, the thing that decides your result is not the dose. It is whether the handful of bites you eat each meal are protein.
