Ozempic Review 2026: Weight Loss, Cost, Ozempic vs Wegovy

October 20, 2026 · 6 min read · By Chris Hardaway
Photo via Pexels

Ozempic is the brand that made GLP-1 a household word, which is odd, because it is not a weight-loss drug on paper. It is semaglutide approved for type 2 diabetes, dosed up to 2 mg a week, and the weight loss that made it famous is a side effect its label does not claim. Its sibling Wegovy is the same molecule at higher doses with the weight label. In 2026 the two also share a $349 self-pay price. So the useful question is not "does Ozempic work" but "which version of semaglutide should I be asking for."

What Ozempic Is and Who It Is For

Ozempic is a once-weekly semaglutide injection in a multi-dose pen. Doses step from 0.25 mg to 0.5, 1 and 2 mg. It is FDA-approved to improve blood sugar in adults with type 2 diabetes, to reduce the risk of major cardiovascular events in adults with type 2 diabetes and heart disease, and to reduce the risk of kidney disease progression in adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Every indication starts with type 2 diabetes. That is what your insurer checks.

What It Did in Trials

  • Blood sugar (SUSTAIN trials): meaningful A1C reductions at 0.5 and 1 mg, larger at 2 mg, and better than several comparator drugs including sitagliptin and dulaglutide.
  • Weight in people with diabetes: roughly 4 to 6 kg at 1 mg in the SUSTAIN program, more at 2 mg. Real but modest next to the numbers people associate with the name.
  • Weight at Wegovy doses: the same molecule at 2.4 mg produced about 15 percent body-weight loss in STEP 1, and about 20.7 percent at the 7.2 mg HD dose. Those are Wegovy's numbers, not Ozempic's.
  • vs tirzepatide: in SURPASS-2, Mounjaro beat semaglutide 1 mg on both A1C and weight in people with type 2 diabetes.

Cost in 2026

With type 2 diabetes and commercial coverage, Ozempic is usually covered and Novo's savings card can reduce the copay. Without coverage, NovoCare's self-pay price is $349 a month for the 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mg doses, $499 for 2 mg, with a $199 introductory price on the two lowest doses for new patients. Medicare Part D plans generally cover it for diabetes. If your goal is weight and you do not have diabetes, note that Wegovy costs the same $349 at the self-pay price and comes with the label and doses built for that goal.

Side Effects

The standard GLP-1 set: nausea concentrated after dose increases, constipation, diarrhea, reflux, fatigue and a flattened appetite. If you also take insulin or a sulfonylurea, low blood sugar becomes possible and your prescriber may adjust those. Rarer serious risks on the label include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, and a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents. The food side of the common effects is covered in the GLP-1 nausea guide; anything beyond food goes to your prescriber.

Eating on Ozempic

Same rules as every drug in the class, with a blood-sugar twist. Protein first at every meal, refined carbs last or not at all, five small eating windows instead of three big ones, and a daily protein floor near 100 grams so the weight that comes off is fat.   • vs Mounjaro: the other diabetes-labeled option; tirzepatide won the direct comparison on both A1C and weight. See the Mounjaro review.

How People Actually Get Ozempic in 2026

There are three routes, and the right one depends on your insurance and your diagnosis.

  1. Through your own doctor and insurance. Ozempic is labeled for type 2 diabetes, so plans generally require that diagnosis; for weight management alone, ask about Wegovy instead. Ask your prescriber what your plan requires; prior authorization is common.
  2. Self-pay direct from the manufacturer. NovoCare Pharmacy ships Ozempic at the $349 self-pay price ($499 for 2 mg), with a $199 introductory price on the lowest doses. Prices change often, so check the manufacturer's page before you decide.
  3. Through an online weight-loss clinic. A licensed clinician reviews your history by video or questionnaire, prescribes if appropriate, and either sends the prescription to a pharmacy or ships treatment to you. This is the fastest route if your regular doctor will not prescribe for weight loss.

FAQ

How much weight do people lose on Ozempic?

Ozempic's diabetes trials (SUSTAIN) showed weight loss of roughly 4 to 6 kg at 1 mg, with more at the 2 mg dose. The same molecule at Wegovy's 2.4 mg dose produced about 15 percent body-weight loss in people without diabetes. Lower dose and a diabetic population both mean less weight loss on Ozempic than the headlines suggest.

Ozempic or Wegovy for weight loss?

Wegovy. It is the same semaglutide at higher doses with a weight-management label, which means insurers will consider it for weight loss, the trial data is for weight loss, and the 7.2 mg HD dose exists. Ozempic for weight loss alone is off-label and rarely covered.

What does Ozempic cost without insurance in 2026?

Novo Nordisk's self-pay price through NovoCare is $349 a month for the 0.25, 0.5 and 1 mg doses, with a $199 introductory price on the two lowest doses for new patients, and $499 for the 2 mg dose. With diabetes coverage, the savings card can bring copays down substantially.

Is Ozempic stronger than Mounjaro?

No. In a direct trial in people with type 2 diabetes, tirzepatide (Mounjaro) lowered A1C more and produced more weight loss than semaglutide 1 mg. Both are effective diabetes drugs; tirzepatide is the stronger of the two on both measures.

The Bottom Line

Ozempic is a very good type 2 diabetes drug that happens to cause weight loss, and it became the household name for the entire category. If you have type 2 diabetes, it is a reasonable first choice and usually covered. If you want it for weight alone, Wegovy is the same molecule in the form built for that, with higher doses, better trial data for weight, and a label insurers accept. Whichever pen you end up with, the appetite drop is the same, and so is the job of filling a small plate with protein.
