A Simple 7-Day Ozempic Meal Plan for Semaglutide Users (2026)

November 9, 2026 · 7 min read · By Chris Hardaway
Photo via Pexels

You have the prescription for Ozempic, Wegovy, or Zepbound. The appetite is gone. The scale is moving. The one thing nobody gives you is a simple instruction manual for how to eat now. A regular diet plan won't work. The portions are too big and the priorities are wrong.

On a GLP-1 medication, a meal plan isn't about recipes. It's a strategy to protect your muscle mass while you lose fat. Rapid weight loss without enough protein pulls from your muscles, slowing your metabolism and making you weaker. This is not a risk; it is a certainty.

This 7-day plan is that strategy. It's built on small, protein-dense meals that add up to the 100-120 grams of protein per day you need to preserve muscle. It's simple, it's repetitive, and it works with a tiny appetite.

The Two Rules of Eating on Semaglutide

Forget everything you know about traditional dieting. There are only two rules that matter now:

  1. Eat 100-120 grams of protein per day. This is not a goal; it's your job. This number provides the building blocks your body needs to maintain muscle and metabolic function during weight loss. We'll aim for about 30 grams per meal and another 10-20 grams from a snack.
  2. Eat your protein first. At every meal, the first bites you take must be the protein source. The chicken, the fish, the eggs, the yogurt. Your appetite might vanish after four or five bites. Make them count. The vegetables and carbs are secondary.

On Ozempic, a meal plan is not a suggestion. It is the instruction manual for keeping your muscle while the fat disappears. Following it is as important as taking your shot.

The Meal Structure: 30-30-30-20

The easiest way to hit 110 grams of protein is to break it down. Think of your day as four protein targets:

  • Breakfast: ~30 grams
  • Lunch: ~30 grams
  • Dinner: ~30 grams
  • Snack: ~20 grams

This structure turns an intimidating daily total into four manageable mini-meals. Below is a 7-day plan showing how to hit these numbers with simple, accessible foods. For a complete list of what to stock, see our Ozempic food list.

A Simple 7-Day Ozempic Meal Plan

This plan is designed for realism. It uses leftovers and simple preparations. Feel free to swap days or substitute a different lean protein. The specific food is less important than the protein total.

Day 1

  • Breakfast (32g protein): 1 cup (227g) nonfat Greek yogurt with 1/2 scoop unflavored protein powder stirred in.
  • Lunch (28g protein): 1 can (5 oz) tuna in water, mixed with 2 tbsp Greek yogurt and seasonings. Eat with 5 whole-grain crackers.
  • Dinner (31g protein): 4 oz baked chicken breast, shredded. Served with 1 cup steamed broccoli and 1/3 cup quinoa.
  • Snack (14g protein): 1/2 cup 2% cottage cheese.

Day 2

  • Breakfast (25g protein): Scramble made with 1 whole egg + 1/2 cup liquid egg whites and 1 oz low-fat cheese.
  • Lunch (31g protein): Leftover 4 oz shredded chicken breast on a bed of lettuce with light vinaigrette.
  • Dinner (28g protein): 4 oz baked cod with lemon. Served with 1 cup roasted asparagus.
  • Snack (14g protein): Two part-skim string cheeses.

Day 3

  • Breakfast (32g protein): 1 cup (227g) nonfat Greek yogurt with 1/2 scoop unflavored protein powder.
  • Lunch (25g protein): 4 slices of deli turkey (low sodium) rolled up with a slice of provolone cheese.
  • Dinner (30g protein): 4 oz 93/7 lean ground turkey, browned and crumbled. Mix with 1/2 cup black beans and 1/4 cup salsa.
  • Snack (12g protein): Two hard-boiled eggs.

Day 4

Repeat Day 1, or swap dinner for a different lean protein like pork tenderloin.

  • Breakfast (32g protein): 1 cup (227g) nonfat Greek yogurt with 1/2 scoop unflavored protein powder.
  • Lunch (28g protein): 1 can (5 oz) tuna in water, mixed with 2 tbsp Greek yogurt.
  • Dinner (30g protein): 4 oz pork tenderloin, sliced. Served with 1 cup green beans.
  • Snack (14g protein): 1/2 cup 2% cottage cheese.

Day 5

Repeat Day 2, using leftovers from Day 4's dinner for lunch.

  • Breakfast (25g protein): Scramble made with 1 whole egg + 1/2 cup liquid egg whites and 1 oz low-fat cheese.
  • Lunch (30g protein): Leftover 4 oz pork tenderloin over salad.
  • Dinner (28g protein): 4 oz baked tilapia. Served with 1 cup sauteed spinach.
  • Snack (14g protein): Two part-skim string cheeses.

Day 6

Repeat Day 3, or use a pre-made shake for convenience.

  • Breakfast (30g protein): Pre-made protein shake (check label for ~30g protein, <10g sugar).
  • Lunch (25g protein): 4 slices of deli turkey rolled up with cheese.
  • Dinner (31g protein): 4 oz baked chicken breast. Served with roasted cauliflower and 1/3 cup brown rice.
  • Snack (12g protein): Two hard-boiled eggs.

Day 7

Use this day to finish leftovers or go with the easiest options.

  • Breakfast (32g protein): 1 cup (227g) nonfat Greek yogurt with 1/2 scoop protein powder.
  • Lunch (31g protein): Leftover 4 oz chicken breast from last night's dinner.
  • Dinner (28g protein): Omelet with 3/4 cup egg whites and leftover vegetables and cheese.
  • Snack (14g protein): 1/2 cup 2% cottage cheese.

The Low-Appetite Day Protocol

Some days, especially after a dose increase, your appetite will be zero. Chewing feels like a chore. On these days, do not try to force solid food. Switch to a liquid and soft food protocol to hit your protein minimum:

  • Meal 1: Protein shake (25-30g protein).
  • Meal 2: 1 cup nonfat Greek yogurt or cottage cheese (15-20g protein).
  • Meal 3: 1 cup bone broth (10g protein) sipped throughout the afternoon.
  • Meal 4: Second protein shake (25-30g protein).

This delivers 75-90 grams of protein without requiring a single solid meal. It's not ideal, but it's far better than the 20 grams you might get by trying and failing to eat normally.

FAQ

What if I can't finish the meals in this plan?

That's expected. The goal isn't to clean your plate; it's to get the protein. Eat the protein portion first. If you eat the 4 oz of chicken (31g protein) but leave the broccoli and quinoa, you have succeeded. The small portions are designed to be manageable, but protein always comes first.

Can I just drink my protein instead of eating it?

You can, and on some days you'll need to. Shakes are a tool, especially for breakfast or low-appetite days. However, whole food sources of protein offer more micronutrients and can be more satisfying. Aim for a balance, using shakes to fill gaps rather than replace all solid meals.

Why is there so much repetition in this 7-day plan?

Repetition is realistic. When your appetite is low and energy may be down, you are not going to cook 21 unique, complex meals. This plan is built on a simple rotation of easy-to-prepare foods. This increases the likelihood that you will actually stick to it and hit your protein goals consistently.

Do I need to follow this Ozempic meal plan exactly?

No. Think of this as a template, not a prescription. You can swap chicken for fish, cottage cheese for Greek yogurt, or rearrange the days. The critical parts are the principles: hitting 100-120g of protein daily in small meals, eating protein first, and staying hydrated. The specific foods are flexible.

The Bottom Line

This 7-day meal plan is a framework, not a set of rigid rules. Its purpose is to show you what a day of eating to protect muscle on Ozempic looks like: small, frequent, and protein-forward. The specific foods are less important than the daily protein total. Your appetite will not be a reliable guide, so a plan—and a way to track your intake against it—is essential. Focus on the protein number, and the rest of the weight loss journey will be healthier and more sustainable.
